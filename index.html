modified Beer-Lambert Law (mBLL)

  • Models changes in light absorption of underlying tissue using continuous waves methods
  • Based on a generalization of Beer-Lambert law
  • Assumes that the scattering properties of tissue do not vary with time

Why fNIRS

  • Commercially available (still pricey)
  • Relatively easy capping
  • Flexible and portable, robust to movement*
  • In development: Hight density, DOT

Limitations

  • Limited access to sub-cortical regions (~3 cm)1
  • Temporal resolution constrained by haemodynamics
  • Limited spatial resolution

  1. But see Diffuse Ooptical Tomography (DOT) (Culver et al. 2001)

Designing a preprocessing pipeline

  • Data quality/inclusion trade-off
  • Performance of all pipelines deteriorated with increasing levels of noise
  • Automatic correction of motion artifacts should be used cautiously, could result in false negatives, reduced effect sizes and an underestimation of the hemodynamic response
  • Applying pre-processing to optical densities or concentration changes achieved overall very similar results

Designing a preprocessing pipeline

Pipeline suggestion

  1. Transform to optical density
  2. Signal quality assessment
  3. Channel pruning
  4. Motion arctifact correction
  5. Transform to ΔHbO and ΔHbR concentrations
  6. Frequency filtering (physiological arctifact attenuation)
  7. Epoching and averaring
  8. Group-level data analysis

Signal quality: after data collection

Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR): Ratio of the average over variability of the signal.

\[ \text{SNR} = \frac{\bar{X}_{\lambda_i}}{\sigma_{X_{\lambda_i}}} \]

Signal quality: after data collection

Scalp coupling index (SCI): Index of optode coupling with scalp based on cardiac pulse (Pollonini et al. 2014)

  1. Band-pass filter the signal in the heartbeat frequency of interest1
  2. Normalize time series and segment into rolling windows (e.g., 5 seconds)
  3. For each window: Zero-lag cross-correlation between time series in both wavelengths
  4. Compare against threshold

\[ \text{SCI} = \rho_{X_{\lambda_1}X_{\lambda_2}}(t=0) = \frac{{X_{\lambda_1}}_{t} - \bar{X}_{\lambda_1} , {X_{\lambda_2}}_{t} - \bar{X}_{\lambda_2}}{\sigma_{\lambda_1}\sigma_{\lambda_2}} \]

  1. 0.5-2.5 Hz in healthy adults, 1.17-3.17 Hz in neonates

Signal quality: channel pruning

Motion arctifact identification

Motion arctifacts (MA) are caused my changes optode position due to movement (cap slides around or separates from scalp).

Brigadoi et al. (2014):

  • Spikes: fast, high amplitude changes
  • Baseline shifts
  • Drift: low frequency variations

Motion arctifact attenuation

  • External signal:
    • Short channel separation
    • Accelerometer (Cui et al. 2015)
  • Correction methods
    • Wavelet filtering (Molavi and Dumont 2012)
    • Temporal deivative distribution repair (TDDR) (Fishburn et al. 2019)
    • Savitzky-Golay spline interpolation (Jahani et al. 2018)
    • Principal component analysis (PCA)

Motion arctifact attenuation

Current recommendation for pediatric data: combination of wavelet filtering and spline interpolation (Di Lorenzo et al. 2019).

For neonatal populations, we found TDDR + wavelet performed best.

    rec.stim,  # stimulus dataframe

# subtract baseline
